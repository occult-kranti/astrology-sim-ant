Calendars & directions

Compare dates, calculate prayer times, and find your bearing. Each result names the convention behind it.

One instant, several calendars

Enter a UTC instant and choose the time zone whose civil date you want to compare. Supported years: 1900–2100.

Use a time-zone name such as Asia/Kolkata, Europe/London, or UTC. Changing it can change the civil day.

These are civil dates with a midnight boundary. Hebrew and Islamic religious days begin at sunset; this display does not determine that boundary or local observances. Browser calendar data can vary. An unsupported method is shown as unavailable.

Prayer times & Qibla

Calculated times for a named method and location. Compare with your community's timetable; local authorities may apply additional adjustments.

Example location: London. West and south coordinates are negative. Coordinates stay in this browser.

High-latitude and Isha settings

Where sunrise or sunset is absent, unresolved events stay unavailable. No substitute location or date is chosen. Moonsighting Committee uses its own seasonal twilight rules. Umm al-Qura uses 90 minutes after sunset; enter +30 only when your chosen Ramadan convention calls for 120 minutes. No Ramadan adjustment is automatic.

Qibla is an initial great-circle bearing clockwise from true north. This page does not use a compass or motion sensors. Magnetic compass readings need a local declination correction. The bearing is unavailable at the Kaaba, its antipode, and geographic poles.

Julian, Gregorian & JD

Proleptic calendar arithmetic. Historical adoption dates differed by place; no reform gap is imposed.

Years 0001–9999 CE. Julian dates can include leap days that Gregorian dates do not.

JD changes at noon UTC. JDN is the integer assigned to that civil date's noon. This arithmetic treats UTC as a uniform day and does not model leap seconds or convert to terrestrial time (TT).

Easter by two conventions

Compare Gregorian and Julian ecclesiastical computus. These rules use a tabular ecclesiastical Moon.

Results are shown as Gregorian civil dates, with the Julian date alongside. A church's actual observance follows its own adopted convention; “Orthodox” does not name one universal rule.

Methods & sources

All calculations run locally. Calendar conversion reuses the Workbench's Julian-day arithmetic. Civil displays use your browser's Intl.DateTimeFormat with verified method IDs. Prayer times use a locally bundled, MIT-licensed Adhan 4.4.6 source snapshot, with a documented UTC date adaptation.

  • US Naval Observatory: calendars and Easter conventions.
  • Hebcal independent Hebrew reference date and Hong Kong Observatory 2026 Chinese calendar.
  • Adhan's method descriptions and MIT license.

These tools do not supply a complete regional Panchang, festival calendar, Jain calendar, or Sikh calendar. For existing instant Jyotisha elements see Muhūrta; instant elements alone do not establish observance dates. Research and verification: 2026-10-03.
