# Country-history source review: India, Russia and Iran

Reviewed 30 October 2026 for the historical country/calendar comparison. This is bounded AI source research and data implementation, not a claim of cultural authority, a complete political history, or a unique national birth. The method separates an enacted date, adoption, commencement, sovereignty declaration, referendum span and republic proclamation. Every encoded record is a `historical-source-assertion` and remains **date-only**. No event UTC instant, midnight, noon, ascendant or exact house configuration is inferred.

Owned implementation: [country-history-east.js](../assets/js/core/data/country-history-east.js). Source access, byte hashes, actual reading scope and failed retrievals: [source-access.json](evidence/country-history/east/source-access.json). PDFs and rendered page images were inspected only in temporary storage and are not redistributed.

## The eight selected markers

| Record | Gregorian reference date | What the checked source establishes | Precision and corroboration |
|---|---|---|---|
| India: appointed independence day | 1947-08-15 | Indian Independence Act 1947, enacted §1(1)–(2), establishes independent Dominions from the appointed day. | One directly checked primary legal authority; independent passage corroboration remains pending. The Act’s 18 July royal assent is a different event. |
| India: constitution adopted | 1949-11-26 | Official constitutional Preamble explicitly adopts the constitution on this date. | Official 2024 consolidated text plus a separate PIB retrospective paragraph agree. They are not two contemporary witnesses. |
| India: constitutional commencement/republic marker | 1950-01-26 | Article 394 appoints this date for the **remaining provisions**; specified provisions came into force earlier. | Official text plus PIB historical summary. The qualification prevents mislabelling this as the first effective day of every provision. |
| Russia: RSFSR sovereignty declaration | 1990-06-12 | The First Congress of People’s Deputies declaration is dated this day and envisages a renewed USSR. | One documentary lineage, reproduced by an academic project; original period issue and FBIS volume not independently retrieved. It does not mark the USSR’s dissolution. |
| Russia: constitution adopted | 1993-12-12 | Section Two, paragraph 1 explicitly names the national referendum day as adoption. | Constitute’s 2014 constitutional reproduction agrees with WIPO legal metadata. WIPO separately records entry into force on 25 December. |
| Iran: republic referendum’s last day | 1979-03-31 | The narrative describes voting on **30–31 March**; the Persian constitution gives **10–11 Farvardin 1358**. | A selected closing date within a two-day event, with no closing hour. The original and translated calendar discrepancy is retained below. |
| Iran: Islamic Republic proclaimed | 1979-04-01 | The country-study narrative reports the proclamation on this date. | Single successfully read historical narrative via a named mirror; original scan and independent official passage remain unavailable. |
| Iran: recorded constitutional entry into force | 1979-12-03 | WIPO metadata records this commencement day. | The legal label is attributed to WIPO. A separate country-study narrative supports the **2–3 December referendum span**, not independently the legal commencement label. |

All markers predate the review cutoff, 2026-10-30. Their selection is documentary and editorial, not a representative sample of all possible national reference events. Earlier state histories, territorial changes, later amendments and many competing reference dates are outside this small set.

## Sources actually inspected

1. **UK enacted legislation:** [Indian Independence Act 1947](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Geo6/10-11/30/enacted), c.30, §1. The inspected heading and appointed-day passage establish the difference between royal assent and the independence marker. No ceremony clock was adopted.
2. **Indian issuing-authority publication:** [Constitution, official 2024 pocket diglot edition](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s380537a945c7aaa788ccfcdf1b99b5d8f/uploads/2024/07/20240716890312078.pdf), as on 1 May 2024. Title and preface identify the Ministry of Law and Justice, Legislative Department, Official Languages Wing; Preamble is PDF p.32; Article 394 is PDF p.283. These passages were text-extracted and read. The entire constitution was not read and the original 1949 manuscript was not inspected.
3. **Separate Indian institutional account:** [PIB, Constitution of India (Interesting Facts)](https://pib.gov.in/FactsheetDetails.aspx?Id=148584&lang=1&reg=3), 25 November 2021, opening paragraph. The broad commencement summary is qualified by the actual Article 394 language. A Constitute 2016 reproduction was also read during discovery, but it is another reproduction of the same constitutional text and is not counted as a new independent primary witness.
4. **Russian primary-text reproduction:** [MSU, Russian State Sovereignty](https://soviethistory.msu.edu/1991/eltsin-and-russian-sovereignty/eltsin-and-russian-sovereignty-texts/russian-state-sovereignty/), document heading, opening and paragraphs 6–7. The project credits *Argumenty i fakty*, no.24 (1990), p.1, and *Foreign Broadcast Information Service, Daily Report*, US Department of Commerce/NTIS, 1991. The declaration explicitly describes a renewed union and continuing USSR relationships. An archived copy of the old MSU URL was also inspected; current and archived copies share a documentary lineage.
5. **Russian constitutional reproduction and legal metadata:** [Constitute, Russia 1993, revised 2014](https://www.constituteproject.org/constitution/Russia_2014?lang=en), Section Two, paragraph 1; [WIPO Lex RU342](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/21035), date fields in its 2020-consolidation record. The primary-text reproduction names adoption; the WIPO fields distinguish adoption and commencement. These sources were not mislabelled as direct retrieval of the 1993 official publication.
6. **Iran country-study narrative, through a mirror:** [The New Constitution](https://countrystudies.us/iran/24.htm), opening and Assembly of Experts paragraphs. The [mirror index](https://countrystudies.us/iran/) names Helen Chapin Metz, editor, *Iran: A Country Study* (Washington: GPO for the Library of Congress, 1987). Original Library of Congress PDF requests returned 403. The page’s explicit institutional attribution and mirror status remain attached; the mirror is not presented as the original archive.
7. **Iranian primary calendar wording:** [WIPO Lex IR001](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/7697), its linked Persian `ir001fa.pdf`, Article 1 continuation, printed/PDF p.9. This passage was extracted and visually inspected in a rendered page. It gives the referendum as 10–11 Farvardin 1358 Solar Hijri, corresponding to 1–2 Jumada al-Ula 1399 lunar Hijri. It does **not** give a Gregorian equivalent. The Gregorian 30–31 March span is separately printed in the country-study narrative. This is selected-passage reading, not full constitutional reading.
8. **Iranian English variant:** [Constitute, Iran 1979, revised 1989](https://www.constituteproject.org/constitution/Iran_1989), Article 1. It prints 9–10 Farvardin and 29–30 March. Those values conflict with the Persian passage and selected narrative. The disagreement is explicit in the record and source catalogue; this translation is not the selected Gregorian authority.
9. **Iranian commencement metadata:** [WIPO Lex IR019](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/14260), 1979-version date fields, names entry into force as 3 December 1979. The IR001 record repeats it, but those two entries share one issuing institution. The Bern ICL text also repeats the metadata and describes a substantially edited embassy-based translation; shared metadata is not counted as an independent historical authority.

## Preserved uncertainties

The Iranian proclamation record has no freshly verified original Solar Hijri day/month label. The commencement metadata likewise does not state an original Solar Hijri day/month. Those fields say so explicitly instead of reverse-converting a Gregorian date and presenting the result as historical documentary evidence. WIPO’s 24 October 1979 adoption field differs in legal stage from the narrative’s assembly-draft completion on 15 November and referendum on 2–3 December; their relationship was not resolved, so no 24 October event is encoded.

No regional Indian lunar/festival calendar is substituted for the statutory Gregorian dates. Indian national Saka and runtime Persian labels produced elsewhere in the app are **calculated civil conventions**. They are distinct from the source’s printed label and are not newly validated across historical ranges by these passages. The reviewed project calendar profiles retain selected-fixture/runtime limitations.

New Delhi, Moscow and Tehran are deliberately rounded **editorial reference cities**, not surveyed event venues. Tehran is not asserted as the physical site of Khomeini’s proclamation, nor as the sole place where a nationwide referendum occurred. Their current IANA zone names are display conventions; no historic offset or exact event instant is established by the coordinates. Any later study sample must identify its chosen clock and observer independently.

## Access, rights and verification

All network requests used the inherited configured proxy and verified TLS. Several responses were unusable despite HTTP 200: the Indian Legislative Department page returned only an application shell; the Russian Presidential Library returned a browser challenge; the Russian Foreign Ministry returned a request-rejected page. Kremlin returned 503, the India Code PDF timed out, and several official Iranian or Indian endpoints were unavailable. The retained ledger distinguishes those states from actual passage inspection. Search snippets and unrelated discovery pages are not cited as checked historical evidence.

The repository retains metadata, bounded excerpts, paraphrased facts and hashes, not full PDFs or copyrighted artwork. Hashes bind the fetched response bytes; they do not certify historical truth, permanent availability or independent provenance. The Persian PDF was retrieved using the actual page-provided iframe URL; an expiring signed query is deliberately not retained. The stable WIPO record remains the citation.

Initial headless verification imported the module, checked eight unique record IDs, referenced source resolution, the three/two/three country distribution, dates on or before the review cutoff, `date-only` with null local clocks, claim type, source citation strings, semantic event labels and explicit approximate-city precision. It passed. No calendar engine was changed. Root integration owns canonical engine/browser checks, cross-review and the held-candidate round; this source report does not claim those checks are complete.
