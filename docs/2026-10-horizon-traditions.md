# Selected traditions and end-time references

This reference layer contains **11 named family scopes and 21 source records**. It makes no dated eschatological prediction for 30 October 2026–1 January 2031. Festival calculations, astronomical events, modern editorial purpose rankings and attributed religious claims are separate products. None of these tradition records contributes to a rank.

Work started from published baseline `3f76845f2d516d7a4bd507afa0508e6929c831e2` on `feat/event-horizon-2031`. This is the source member of a six-agent review, not six human religious authorities. The shared source-contract, accuracy-check and date-event-search-review skills were read and applied. Source reading, independent corroboration, numerical verification, community endorsement and publication remain different statuses.

## Findings that affect implementation

### No invented 2031 apocalypse

Every row has `datedClaims: []` and `contributesToRanking: false`. Statuses distinguish **undated**, **non-dateable**, **not-assessed**, and **historical-not-current**. These are editorial evidence statuses, not degrees of belief or predictions of absence. No checked source in this selected corpus establishes a dated apocalypse in the requested horizon; the corpus is not all religious literature.

The strongest positive date-setting cautions are in Mark 13:32–37, Qur’an 7:187/31:34, Sanhedrin 97b:9 and Maimonides 12:2. Sanhedrin’s historical failed-date criticism is preserved as such, without pretending all rabbinic speakers agree. The contemporary Adventist statement is current official teaching; no unsupported claim about a specific nineteenth-century date is inserted. A guessed historical-article URL returned 404 and was not treated as evidence.

### A real disagreement with pan-religious scoring

SGPC Article XVI(d), p.36, explicitly rejects beliefs in auspicious times, days and occasions, influence of stars and horoscopic dispositions. The source is a scanned 64-page PDF. `pdftotext` produced only 66 characters, so that extraction was not counted as reading. Selected pages were rendered and OCRed; the critical p.36 image was then visually inspected to verify the wording. The translator’s preface identifies Kulraj Singh and is dated 31 August 1994. The PDF metadata’s 2023 creation date and the host’s 2026 upload path are not conflated with the work’s first adoption date.

This evidence was sent to the ranking owner. Their contract excludes observance/religion/eschatology inputs and calls purpose selections and weights modern editorial experiments. A Sikh festival may appear as an observance without being misrepresented as support for an astrological ranking. The same separation applies to the other families, regardless of whether the selected passage explicitly discusses astrology.

### A textual astronomical condition is not a validated event solver

Bhāgavata 12.2.24 is genuinely interesting for comparisons: the retrieved BBT translation names the Moon, Sun and Bṛhaspati, Karkaṭa and Puṣya. The data therefore preserves the named bodies and terms in `textualConfiguration`. It also records the missing validation: epoch/frame convention, precise simultaneous-entry meaning, independent textual/celestial interpretation and a justified modern date association. A recurring angular pattern does not establish fulfillment, predict a catastrophe, or produce a justified ranked day. No yuga-duration constant or epoch was added from memory.

The Bahá’í chapter’s historical astronomical illustration also remains an attributed argument. Its numerical statements about stars are **not** imported into the modern astronomy engine. Historical text and measured astronomy do not become interchangeable because both mention the sky.

### Calendars are not end-time dates

The official Bahá’í introduction was independently read by this source role and the observance role. It explains sunset-to-sunset days, equinox-linked Naw-Rúz and the Twin Birthdays after the eighth new moon. The observance owner investigates published dated tables; this role does not claim to validate them. No universal fixed 21 March rule is substituted.

Jinja Honcho explicitly says local shrine traditions vary. SGPC discipline pages do not constitute a Nanakshahi festival table. Jain philosophical cosmology does not calculate sect-specific festivals. The Saka and Thai Buddhist civil labels do not, by themselves, implement Hindu or Buddhist festival rules. The observational and computed Islamic calendars likewise need named variants. These statements describe implementation coverage, not a denial of community calendars.

The observance owner independently found a **2027 Chinese-date disagreement** between the runtime conversion and freshly checked Hong Kong official dates. That finding belongs to their evidence and algorithms, not this module; it reinforces why civil conversion, published observance and doctrinal reference must keep their provenance separate.

## Rights, uncertainty and failed access

Sefaria’s returned metadata identifies **CC-BY-NC** for the William Davidson Sanhedrin edition and Touger’s Maimonides translation. These terms are not promoted to unrestricted training/republication rights. The historical Darmesteter translation is public domain, with host/editor credited. Modern BBT, Mukundananda, USCCB, scholarly and community editions retain their rights. The module uses original concise paraphrases rather than lengthy quotations.

SuttaCentral’s normal text and licensing URLs returned application shells. Only the separate public API supplied the actually read DN 26 segments. This pass does not claim that the shell verified the translation’s licence. The shipped record states that limitation instead of copying a blanket licence from another SuttaCentral layer.

Access/discovery outcomes retained in the receipt include:

- Two guessed Chabad chapter URLs redirected to a 404 page; the successful Sefaria API was then used for the named text.
- `old.sgpc.net` failed with a tunnel 403; the current official `sgpc.net` page linked the successfully retrieved PDF. No proxy bypass was used.
- The guessed SikhRI end-of-world page and SikhiWiki page returned 404 and did not become source evidence.
- JAINA’s guessed page returned 403. A guessed JAINpedia universe path returned 404; a guessed time path redirected to an unrelated manuscript record. The actual scholarly universe article was discovered from the site’s own search and read separately.
- A guessed Jinja Honcho festivals path returned 404; its actual Matsuri page succeeded.
- The guessed Adventist historical article returned 404. The separately successful current official belief page supports only its stated contemporary teaching.
- Initial Bahá’í numbered pages were discovery/contents material. Chapter 26 was actually found and read in page 7, with its exact chapter locator retained.

An HTTP failure here is evidence about this request, not proof the work is unavailable everywhere. Two mirrors or translations of a text do not become independent confirmation of its theology. Single-source attributions remain explicitly bounded; where two scholarly accounts agree, that is still not empirical evidence of a religious future event.

## Interface and review status

`assets/js/core/data/horizon-traditions.js` exports deeply frozen `HORIZON_TRADITIONS`, `HORIZON_TRADITION_SOURCES` and `HORIZON_TRADITION_COVERAGE`. Family IDs coordinate with the observance owner. Source IDs use `HTR-`; observance-source IDs use `HOBS-`. Every row carries scope, a concise summary, an eschatology status, calendar notes, limitations, and exact source references. The UI should display them in a separate attributed-reference section, never inject them into a date rank or claim they are missing “votes.”

R1 source data and fresh receipts are implemented. The [R1 source-contract receipt](evidence/horizon/traditions/source-checks-r1.json) records **37 passing checks**: exact family coverage, unique identifiers, resolvable source references, bounded/non-dated/non-ranking statuses, deep freezing, source-to-retrieval fingerprints, exact locators and preservation of failed/shell-only responses. These are metadata integrity checks, not proof of theological truth or community acceptance. The receipt records the file hashes at that check; later documentation additions do not rewrite that historical record.

The source role independently read the actual ranking module, method report and retrieval receipt for R2. Its four profiles preserve fixed complete denominators, null missing/untimed yoga, raw historical classifications, declared shared-input groups and zero religious/event contributions. The review additionally inspected Agrippa II.32’s opening and II.43, plus Lilly’s selected pp.77–78/115 in the already available response files whose hashes match the ranking owner’s fresh retrieval. The associations support a qualified study theme, not the modern weights, reversal for completion, or a claim of improved learning. One source-metadata correction was requested: explicitly identify the modern Lilly transcription’s ©2003 World Astrology Network edition and retained modern rights, rather than allowing public-domain historical text to imply unrestricted rights to the transcription. No scoring change was requested by this source review.

The ranking owner applied that edition/rights correction to the module, method report and source receipt. A read-only follow-up confirmed it; the reviewed ranking-module SHA-256 was `af2c5ff2550e2c65392f99c46d00e3ad5abb8baddcf5e36dca45fc813435ba08`. This closes the source qualification finding without changing scores.

Later held-candidate acceptance, browser review, complete-range stress checks and publication are separate gates. Successful research fetches and source-contract checks alone do not establish them.

The exact 11 tradition rows and all 21 tradition-source records in the preset match the current authored module, including rights, selected reading depth, empty dated claims and exclusion from ranking.

`scripts/generate-horizon-preset.mjs` uses the canonical optimized builder. Its source graph uses Node’s actual static-module parser, not a text pattern, and includes transitive relative dependencies from the core orchestrator and tool inventory. The generated version module is excluded to avoid a hash cycle. The build aborts if source bytes change during calculation. Its 13 integrity self-checks exercised checksum/metadata tampering, missing date/calendar/profile rows, wrong observer context, false score accounting, incorrect observance ownership, truncation and version parsing. One initial self-check caught a property-order-sensitive object comparison; it was corrected to semantic deep comparison before generating any data.

The full build took 51.432 seconds on Node 24.19.0 / ICU 78.3 / CLDR 48.0 / tz 2026b. Four compact parts plus the manifest total **35,146,686 bytes**. The output contains 1,525 dates per case, 4,575 samples, 59,475 calendar slots, 18,300 purpose results, 17,114 rankable results, 1,186 explicit unrankable results, 1,011 observance rows (337 unique source observances), and 5,883 astronomical events. No requested row was truncated. `--check` verified committed bytes, complete counts, source/metadata/catalogue bindings and the generated version without repeating the expensive calculation. The actual preset loader also accepted those bytes through a local file transport; this is not a substitute for browser/network acceptance.
