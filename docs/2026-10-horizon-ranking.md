# Purpose profiles and tool coverage

The event horizon ranks an explicit sampled instant under one named **modern editorial study profile**. It does not rank religions, divine efficacy, actual future outcomes or whole days. The default cases have different observer-local noons and therefore different UTC instants. They are separate comparison populations.

`assets/js/core/horizon-ranking.js` exposes `HORIZON_PURPOSES`, `HORIZON_RANKING_SOURCES` and `evaluateHorizonPurposes({comparison,guidance})`. The root pipeline supplies fresh canonical `compareMoment` and `buildDayGuidance` results. This pure evaluator transforms those records; it is not a second astronomy engine or an authentication mechanism for arbitrary caller-provided charts. Public adapters must recompute their records from explicit moment inputs. A differing instant, zone, observer or method context is rejected rather than merged across cases.

## Declared profiles

| Profile | Inputs and integer weights | Meaning of a higher value |
|---|---|---|
| Western increase study | phase: 2; inherited solar proximity: 2; lunar speed: 2; Via Combusta: 2; applying Mars aspect: 1; applying Saturn aspect: 1 | Greater match to this declared increasing-light preference and inherited rule subset. No complete election or outcome prediction. |
| Western decrease / completion study | The same six contributions and weights; phase preference reversed | Greater match to the editorial decreasing-light/completion study. The original neutral waning guidance class remains visible; Lilly's table is not rewritten. |
| Inherited Jyotiṣa classification study | tithi: 1; karaṇa: 1; yoga: 2; nakṣatra: 2 | Higher weighted class index within the existing secondary-source classification tables. Primary-passage verification remains pending; no complete muhūrta or festival decision. |
| Mercury-hour study match | one temporal-hour match: 1 | Mercury ruler = 1; any other known ruler = 0. This is a correspondence-reading preference, not a bad-hour label or an implemented image procedure. |

For inherited classes, favorable maps to +1, neutral to 0 and caution to −1. For the two Western phase terms, the selected phase maps to +1 and its opposite to −1; this is expressly a profile preference, not a new source class. Mercury hour uses its separate 1/0 mapping. The weighted numerator is divided by the **fixed complete denominator** 10, 10, 6 or 1 and multiplied by 100. Scores are index points bounded by −100 and 100, never percentages or probabilities. Physical and classification constraints mean both endpoints need not be attainable. Different profiles share a display scale but are not interchangeable measurements.

The Western profile assigns a total budget 4 to the shared elongation group, 2 to lunar speed, 2 to lunar tropical longitude and 2 to the two Moon–planet contact terms. That gives the explicitly chosen phase/solar relation greater editorial importance; it is not two independent confirmations. The Jyotiṣa profile assigns one total budget 2 to tithi and karaṇa together, because both partition the same elongation, and budget 2 each to sidereal Sun-plus-Moon longitude and sidereal Moon longitude. These groups still share the underlying Sun/Moon observations. No independence, correlation significance, confidence or majority vote is inferred.

Every selected contribution must have an observed class, required measured inputs and a known source. Missing/unavailable rules, absent measurements or untimed opening-portion yoga produce `rankable:false`, `score:null` and `scoreNumerator:null`; the full denominator remains. Removing an adverse observation therefore cannot improve a score. The evaluator exposes `observed`, `total`, `missing`, individual weighted contributions and dependency groups. Contributions preserve original classifications, numerical measurements, source status and rule-specific limitations. A favorable and cautionary testimony may coexist even when the editorial sum is positive.

Equal exact integer numerators under the same denominator within a profile/case are ties. The root orchestrator owns their ranks, tie counts and stable date order. Date order within a tie expresses no stronger result. All dates and missing samples must remain in the underlying ledger; selecting top rows for presentation is not a licence to discard unavailable dates from coverage.

## Source reading and disagreement

On 30 October 2026, the ranking review freshly retrieved Agrippa's 1651 English text in Peterson's digital edition and the World Astrology Network transcription of Lilly Book I. The latter is a modern transcription marked copyright 2003, not a facsimile; the historical work's public-domain status does not erase the modern edition notice. Only original paraphrases are stored, and no PDF or modern editorial content is redistributed. The access receipt, hashes, actual reading depth and unusable Skyscript response are in [ranking-sources.json](evidence/horizon/ranking-sources.json). No complete-book reading, human practitioner validation or empirical efficacy study occurred.

Selected Agrippa II.29 requires several celestial conditions together; a single phase or hour is not a sufficient rendition. II.32's opening associates the Moon with generation, increase and decrease. Lilly's p. 115 table distinguishes increasing light as fortitude and decreasing light as debility. These are related claims, not an endorsement by both authors of a modern completion profile. The decrease/completion task choice and all numerical weights are authored experiments.

Agrippa II.43 reports Mercury-image knowledge/eloquence associations with additional hour and ascendant conditions. Lilly I pp. 77–78 associates Mercury with learning and literate professions under stated conditions. This supports a qualified historical association, not evidence that a Mercury hour improves studying. The profile does not implement the image, dignity or ascendant conditions. Agrippa II.34 reports the twelve daylight/twelve night convention implemented here and also a different ascension-based convention, which remains unimplemented.

The inherited guidance engine still uses its documented solar-distance convention, lunar-speed thresholds and 0.05-day linear applying-aspect test. These profiles do not silently substitute the newer sky-reading engine's textual same-sign solar convention. Nor does fresh reading of the selected Lilly pages validate every other inherited rule. The inherited Jyotiṣa quality tables retain their secondary-source status; a tithi arithmetic reference would not validate those quality assignments.

Religious observance and eschatology records are reference layers. They have no ranking contributions. In particular, a tradition's rejection of auspicious-time or horoscope beliefs must not be converted into a supposedly universal cross-religious score. The source owner's `HTR-SIKH-REHAT` record documents that distinction separately.

## Every tool is inventoried; every tool is not a date scanner

`core/data/horizon-tool-coverage.js` joins an explicit authored assignment to the actual generated MCP schemas. Each registered tool retains its exact required fields and available input names; conditional-mode requirements still require the full MCP schema and semantic validator. An unreviewed new tool is visibly `not-reviewed`, and the focused test rejects a released inventory with that gap.

The inventory distinguishes engines actually used by the pipeline, an explicitly limited shared-engine subset, date-capable but unexecuted tools, separate bounded scanners, inputs needing a target/operation, birth/personal or question context, explicit oracle draws, text/design choices, historical references/reconstructions and the curated event agenda. It additionally lists the nineteen existing reference/rendering capabilities so layout helpers and book catalogues are not falsely advertised as date-quality engines.

The scan invokes canonical modules directly; **zero MCP adapters are invoked by the scan**. For example, the full muhūrta wrapper is not executed merely because `panchangaScreens` is reused. A horoscope's default quesited house does not establish an actual user question. A future date does not create a person's birth, a random tarot draw or a missing partner. Existing printed-chart reconstruction keys and stored events remain historical/reference records, not computed future outcomes. Canonical computus is cached by observance year and convention, while date conversions and prayer times carry their own boundaries and no favorable/caution verdict.

## Verification

The two read-only adapters in `mcp/horizon-adapters.mjs` recompute this pipeline from strict explicit inputs. `workbench_horizon_scan` accepts at most 31 inclusive dates and two cases, with complete rows and explicitly chosen profiles. `workbench_horizon_day` accepts one date/case and selected profiles, with optional canonical hourly guidance. Every case requires an explicit local sample clock; no birth, incoming chart, ledger or question is accepted. Astronomy defaults off and observance projections default on, both recorded in the normalized request. A 1.3 MB adapter response cap leaves room for the toolkit envelope; an excessive result fails with a narrowing instruction instead of silently dropping rows. The full local horizon remains a separate larger-range workflow.

Focused adapter checks cover canonical semantic parity, actual asynchronous output, all 31 × 2 requested rows, fold missingness and explicit fold choices, measured-rule missingness, actual 25-hour detail, an opt-in solved eclipse, unknown/private fields, sparse/duplicate selections and absence of network access. A New York 25-hour/four-profile day returned 840,797 UTF-8 bytes during the implementation check; this is a measured fixture, not a bound on every possible future response. Hourly guidance is unranked segment evidence and does not invent exact rule-transition times or per-hour purpose scores.

`node scripts/tests/horizon-ranking.mjs` exercises actual canonical fixtures, opposite-phase preference without rewriting raw classes, absent adverse observations, missing measurements/sources, an actual unresolved opening-yoga sample, polar unavailable hours, fixed denominators, explicit dependencies, context mismatch, duplicate rules, unrelated religious/calendar metadata, deterministic output and exact tool-schema inventory. The first implementation passes 15 meaningful groups; later panel/whole-horizon evidence belongs in the release ledger rather than being inferred from this count.

The proposed whole-horizon brute/optimized equivalence and final browser acceptance are separate root/QA gates. Numerical equality is an engineering result, not independent predictive confirmation. No live language-model inference, model training, personal-data upload or hosted service deployment follows from these ranking tests.
