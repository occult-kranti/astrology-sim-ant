# Source-qualified observances through 1 January 2031

Baseline: `3f76845f2d516d7a4bd507afa0508e6929c831e2`, branch `feat/event-horizon-2031`. This is the calendar/observance role of the six-agent horizon panel, not human religious authority. The module accepts **1 January 2026 through 1 January 2031 inclusive**; the initial default horizon starts on 30 October 2026. A date labels a selected convention or publication. It does not establish a universally observed festival, an exact ceremony time or a favorable/caution outcome.

Implementation: [horizon-observances.js](../assets/js/core/horizon-observances.js), [authored source/date catalogue](../assets/js/core/data/horizon-observances.js), and [focused tests](../scripts/tests/horizon-observances.mjs). The implementation reuses canonical Gregorian/Julian arithmetic and `easter()`; it does not replace any existing civil converter.

## Input, output and missingness

`observancesForDate({date, calendars, observer})` returns a bounded array of date-only rows. `date` is a Gregorian civil date; `calendars` is the canonical `compareMoment().calendars` array; `observer` supplies explicit latitude, longitude and an IANA zone. The orchestration/MCP caller must compute those calendar rows from explicit inputs. This internal API is not permission to accept a model's or remote caller's fabricated calculated rows. It checks supported identities, duplicate fields, bounded values and the Gregorian date's consistency; it does not independently recalculate all thirteen supplied converters.

Each output has a stable `id`, `date`, `title`, `familyId`, `variantId`, `sourceIds`, `precision`, `status`, `boundary`, `region`, `method` and `limitations`. Exact start/end timestamps remain `null`. Rows can distinguish calculated date rules, published dates, published-derived dates, provisional calendar-method projections, a narrowly attributed source rule, and unavailable calculations. No output contains an auspiciousness score, probability, geographic affiliation inference or private observer record.

The separate `HORIZON_OBSERVANCE_COVERAGE` catalogue has 27 rows: fourteen observance-method entries and thirteen inherited civil-converter entries. An empty event array means **no selected supported rule matched**, not that no observance occurs. Unsupported communities remain visible in coverage. A missing/unrecognized Hebrew or named Islamic calendar creates an explicit unavailable row instead of a misleading clean non-match. If the sample clock is unavailable, date-only computus, Gregorian/Julian fixed-date arithmetic and published facts can still annotate the requested civil date; no exact clock is synthesized.

Purim uses Adar II in a leap year. Postponed fasts and a complete festival/work-restriction calendar are not implemented. |
| Islamic | Separate `islamic-civil` and `islamic-umalqura` projections for Ramadan's first date, Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, New Year and Ashura; selected MUIS 2027 published dates | Neither runtime convention determines observed crescents or a local authority's decision. Published Singapore dates are a separate regional variant. A Ramadan date is not a fasting-time calculation. |
| Chinese | Twenty-five selected festival/solar-term dates across 2026–2030 from official HKO daily tables: Lunar New Year, Qingming, Tuen Ng, Mid-Autumn and Chung Yeung | Hong Kong convention. Ordinary-month festivals exclude the repeated intercalary month. The public holiday after Mid-Autumn is not confused with the festival itself. Runtime disagreements remain visible. |

| Zoroastrian | Only Avesta.org's expressly fixed **21 March Fasli** Naw Ruz convention | This is a narrowly attributed source rule; independent future date fixtures remain pending. Shahanshahi, Qadimi and equinox-based variants are unavailable. The Persian civil converter is not substituted. |
| Sikh | Official SGPC calendar publication identified; no dates encoded | The retrieved Punjabi calendar has not been independently translated/date-verified in this pass. Nanakshahi/Bikrami and community versions cannot be silently merged. |
| Jain | Selected community festival descriptions catalogued; no Gregorian dates encoded | Named regional month, tithi and Svetambara/Digambara policies need an identified implementation and checked fixtures. An instant tithi does not supply those rules. |
| Shinto | Official Jinja Honcho account of local/seasonal diversity; no universal schedule encoded | Shrine-specific dates are not inferred from Japanese era labels. |

All thirteen inherited civil rows remain separate: Gregorian, Julian, Hebrew, Islamic civil, Umm al-Qura, Chinese, Thai Buddhist, Indian national Saka, Persian, Coptic, Ethiopic, Japanese era and ROC/Minguo. Their original runtime/fixture limitations are not upgraded merely because an observance layer exists. In particular, no Coptic or Ethiopic feast calendar, Persian religious calendar or pan-Asian festival calendar has been invented.

## Sunset is not midnight

Jewish rows use the **daytime Gregorian date**, with the previous civil date explicitly named as the start-date label for sunset-based observance. Exact sunset/nightfall and community-specific services are not calculated. For example, Pesach's first daytime date in 2027 is 22 April, with the beginning identified as sunset on 21 April. A daily sample on 21 April does not become an exact evening religious calendar result.

Islamic projections likewise name a native calendar date and retain unresolved local sunset, sighting and authority decisions. Bahá’í records follow the official sunset-to-sunset convention: Naw-Rúz's published 20 March 2028 daytime date begins at sunset on 19 March. No module-generated UTC start is supplied. Christian fixed dates and regional civil holiday publications also do not imply a ceremony hour.

## Fresh source work and rights

The [source receipt](evidence/horizon/observances/sources.json) preserves **57 URL retrieval records**, including redirects, access failures and unused pages, plus 28 source records with title, publisher, locator, SHA-256, access time, rights and actual reading depth. HTTP success is not treated as passage verification. Examples include a BAPS URL redirected to access-denied content, inaccessible JAINA/UN pages, obsolete URLs and an Islamic Relief calendar page whose returned content did not establish the requested dates. None is used to manufacture a missing schedule.

Selected passages/rows actually inspected:

- [OCA Paschal cycle](https://www.oca.org/fs/paschal-cycle): Western Easter, Pascha, Ascension and Pentecost through 2030; separate Nativity and Theophany commemoration headings. [USCCB 2027](https://www.usccb.org/resources/2027cal.pdf) and [2028](https://www.usccb.org/resources/2028cal.pdf): principal celebrations and the explicit US Ascension jurisdiction split. These are selected calendar readings, not complete liturgical-book study.
- [Hebcal's six-year holiday table](https://www.hebcal.com/holidays/) and its 2027 Diaspora/Israel JSON date records: native dates and differing durations. [Chabad 2027](https://www.chabad.org/holidays/default_cdo/year/2027/jewish/holidays-2027.htm): selected dates, regional qualifications and sunset/nightfall. Evening Hanukkah candle labels are not daytime ordinal labels. Two Hebcal responses are one issuing engine, not independent authorities.
- [HKO 2027 text table](https://www.hko.gov.hk/en/gts/time/calendar/text/files/T2027e.txt) and equivalent 2026–2031 tables: full daily data parsed, selected festival dates inspected. [GovHK 2027](https://www.gov.hk/en/about/abouthk/holiday/2027.htm): named festival/public-holiday cross-checks. HKO PDFs were also retrieved and selected rows read; PDF and text formats from one authority do not count as independent validation.
- [MUIS Islamic calendar resources](https://www.muis.gov.sg/resources/islamic-calendar/): the 2027 key-date sheet and selected February/March calendar-grid cells. The sheet's “Islamic New Year” heading contains `1448H` while its native-date column says `1 Muharram 1449H`; this discrepancy is recorded, not silently propagated. [Singapore MOM](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/public-holidays): the 2026/2027 dated rows and substitute-holiday notes.
- [Official Bahá’í calendar](https://www.bahai.org/action/devotional-life/calendar): introductory method/boundary paragraphs and selected published display; its linked public JavaScript component supplies selected factual year records. Downloaded code was inspected as text and **not executed**. Only factual dates and independently written date arithmetic are retained, not the application's source code. The [10 July 2014 Universal House of Justice message](https://www.bahai.org/library/authoritative-texts/the-universal-house-of-justice/messages/20140710_001/1) supplies the Tehran/equinox, eighth-new-moon, fixed Badí‘ date and sunset rules. These publications share a tradition authority; consistency is not independent astronomical certification.
- [Avesta.org calendar](https://www.avesta.org/zcal.html) and [Iranica, Calendars IV](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/calendars/calendars-iv-other-modern-calendars/): distinct modern Zoroastrian calendar communities and reform history. Iranica's history does not independently establish every future fixed-21-March date, hence the narrow source-rule status.
- [SGPC calendar page](https://sgpc.net/nanakshahi-calendar/), [Jainworld festival descriptions](https://jainworld.com/societies-sanghs/jain-festivals/) and [Jinja Honcho Matsuri](https://www.jinjahoncho.or.jp/en/shinto/matsuri/): metadata or selected descriptions supporting the stated unavailable scope, not fabricated future Gregorian dates.

Complete downloaded source pages, PDFs and third-party application bundles remain outside the repository. The committed files contain factual date extracts, original paraphrases and source metadata. No complete-book reading, new source licence, human tradition review or comprehensive religious authority is claimed.

The [fact-extraction driver](evidence/horizon/observances/extract-source-facts.py) reproduces the selected catalogue from the downloaded files and retrieval receipts, verifying the recorded SHA-256 of every supplied body before extraction. Usage: `python3 docs/evidence/horizon/observances/extract-source-facts.py /path/to/downloaded-source-directory`. Its input filenames are the receipt keys plus `.raw`, with the individual `*-receipts.json` retrieval files. Future source revisions must be reviewed as new inputs rather than substituted into the old receipt.

## Actual runtime disagreement

Fresh official HKO and GovHK sources place Lunar New Year on **6 February 2027**. Node v24.19.0 with ICU **78.3**, CLDR **48.0** and timezone data **2026b** labels local noon that day as Chinese **twelfth month, day 30**, with first month/day 1 on 7 February. For **3 February 2030**, HKO publishes first month/day 1 while the same runtime reports first month/day 2.

The implementation leaves the canonical converter untouched. A published observance carries `calendarComparison`, containing the published native fields, Hong Kong reference zone, actual runtime label, and `matches`, `mismatch` or `unavailable`. Matching selected fields are not a full-range validation. A browser may ship different ICU data from the static artifact generator; neither artifact reproducibility nor a matching program hash establishes calendar authority.

The [Round 1 selected-festival audit](evidence/horizon/observances/round1-runtime-comparison.json) compares all 25 selected HKO dates at explicit local noon in Hong Kong, London, Delhi and New York. Its **100 comparisons comprise 92 matches and 8 mismatches**, the two New Year disagreements in each case, with no unavailable labels. This is a selected-festival audit, not an exhaustive comparison of every daily Chinese conversion. Independent QA reproduced the 2027 source/date mismatch separately.

## Round 2 integration review

CSV is a complete **date ledger for one selected case/profile**, including observance IDs; it is not a standalone export of every festival's source/method record. Those IDs join to the full JSON observance catalogue. ICS previews one available study sample in UTC, omits coordinates and saved births, and explicitly does not describe a religious boundary or connected calendar synchronization. An unavailable sample cannot be exported as an invented instant. Observance context never changes the editorial profile score.

The reviewed source resolves the reported presentation blocker. Final acceptance remains conditional on the held source manifest and integrated QA receipt; no new browser execution or broad test repetition is claimed by this role in Round 2.
