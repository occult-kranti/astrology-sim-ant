# Study lenses: sources, workflows and method boundaries

Research and implementation date: **2026-10-05 UTC**. The source review inspected local Workbench **ce8534b**, the actual source catalogue and pure calculation modules. The lead separately tracked remote PR3 and Pages publication; a local review is not deployment evidence. This addition belongs to `feat/observatory-sessions-2026-10`.

`assets/js/core/data/study-lenses.js` exports the immutable `STUDY_LENSES` array and `getStudyLens(id)`, which returns a matching record or `null`. IDs are `lilly`, `agrippa`, `hermetic`, and `newton`. Each record contains a summary, suggested prompts, curated source records and existing tool links relative to `pages/studio.html`. These are **curated passages, not sources newly retrieved by an AI answer**. Source IDs are stable citation handles; they do not assert that every claim in a book is true or computationally implemented.

This was an actual AI engineering/source review with a separate AI source-skeptic task. No historical author participated, no endorsement is implied and no human peer review is claimed. The `resonance-research-advisor` and `historical-physics-panel` skills informed the review. Their main instructions were accessible; their linked auxiliary reference resources were unavailable. Source assertions, tested software behavior, historical interpretations and unverified physical claims remain separate.

The first coherent feature is the **frozen observation-to-study session**. These lens records are its source layer; they do not themselves implement transfer, journal storage, a model connection or those workflow acceptance tests. The UI and shared session integrations have separate owners and evidence. Basic source access and citation handling belong in the first slice; a broader cross-tradition comparison interface can reuse existing routes without duplicating their engines.

## Planetary-hour convention

Agrippa II.34 first describes ordinary division of sunrise-to-sunset into twelve equal daylight intervals and sunset-to-next-sunrise into twelve equal night intervals. It then reports a contrasting method based on **fifteen degrees of ecliptic ascension**, evaluated through regional oblique-ascension tables. The application’s `core/planetary-hours.js` implements the **ordinary sunrise/sunset division**, with explicit local sunrise-day context. It does not implement the alternative just because a source mentions it.

Use “Planetary hours: sunrise/sunset division,” not “Agrippa’s exact hour method.” At polar dates without the required intervals, the result may be unavailable. `core/election.js` discloses that numeric weights, thresholds and named-aim mappings are application editorial choices, not a canonical Lilly/Picatrix formula or probabilities of outcomes.

## Catalogue validation boundary

The bounded module checks verify four stable lens IDs; nonempty required fields; unique source IDs; HTTPS source links; relative existing tool routes and anchors; immutable records; exact missing-ID behavior; and retention of the competing-hour, source-edition and observation-uncertainty qualifications. These checks validate the catalogue contract and navigation. They do not verify historical efficacy, every linked book, external server uptime or an AI provider’s response.
