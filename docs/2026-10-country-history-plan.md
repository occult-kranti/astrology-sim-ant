# Historical country and calendar studies

Baseline: published `e049fc9dfa51694f254552862f82cd917964676a`; development branch `feat/country-calendar-history`. The preceding2031 event horizon is already deployed and independently verified. This page adds historical source comparison and explicitly assumed historical-day geometry. It does not select dates for war, optimize military actions, rank countries, or score future outcomes.

## Reader flow

1. Select up to six documented historical markers from the United States, Israel, India, Russia, Iran and Palestine. Declaration, legal commencement, constitutional adoption and international status are distinct event types. Selecting one is not evidence of a unique national birth.
2. Compare source precision, original calendar wording, documentary venue or editorial reference city, and named civil conversions. Unknown clocks remain unknown. A disclosed local-noon reference can compare civil labels, but it is not an event timestamp or national nativity.
3. Optionally inspect one recorded historical civil day's sensitivity to assumed times under Regiomontanus, Placidus, Whole Sign or Equal houses. Every sample is labelled an assumption; the full twelve-cusp geometry, day interval, offsets and unavailable clocks remain visible. There is no quality ranking or privileged sample.
4. Read the twelve-house reference, compare calendar/tradition scope and consult peaceful civic/archive task requirements. A Christian book title, Egyptian calendar, Hebrew calendar or Indian national calendar does not establish a universal religious prediction rule.
5. Export the declared selection, provenance, uncertainty and computed samples. Input changes invalidate old results. No stored personal birth, private question or model credential is read. The bounded source MCP adapter accepts only curated record IDs and the disclosed historical options.

## Boundaries

The shared calendar comparison supports both local and UTC years1900–2100. Earlier US records retain their sources with unavailable calculations. No replacement date is invented. Source-only dates, modern IANA zones and approximate city coordinates do not establish exact historical clocks. Civil conversions may be proleptic; their existence does not prove historical adoption or a sunset religious boundary. Source access failures and single-source assertions remain visible.
