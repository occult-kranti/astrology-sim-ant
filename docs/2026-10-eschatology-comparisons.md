# Selected eschatology and chronology comparisons

This module supplies eleven attributed comparisons across nine requested families. It does not claim to cover all religions or every branch of a named tradition. It describes historical texts and organizational statements without assigning nations hidden gods, placing religions on a good/dark scale, or turning a textual calendar into a physical mechanism or outcome forecast.

The source contract, accuracy-check and add-data-module skills were applied. The catalogue review covered relevant entries in `greatworks.js`, `greatworks-east.js`, `opgraph.js`, `kabbalah-data.js`, `study-lenses.js` and `horizon-traditions.js`. Catalogue presence and relationship edges establish neither full-book reading nor a verified chronology rule. Only the expressly identified passages and inherited receipts support the new summaries. No browser was launched and no commit was made by this lane.

## Structured contract

`ESCHATOLOGY_COMPARISONS` contains frozen records with IDs, family labels, literal `source` citations, source IDs, a textual `calendarChronology`, `dateClaim`, scoped `authority`, `variants`, actual `reading` status, and `limitations`. Unknown values remain null. `calculatorRule` is null for all records: this comparison module supplies no forecast, date converter, or score. The separate canonical Newton chronology engine owns its named arithmetic scenarios.

`dateClaim.precision` distinguishes a reported exact civil date, a year, an interval, or an unknown date. The data currently uses a conditional year for Newton, a historical exact-date correspondence for the Maya source, and unknown dates elsewhere. A year must not become January 1; a divine-year duration must not become an anchored interval. Hindu textual quantities retain their units and null epoch separately from the unknown fulfillment date.

`ESCHATOLOGY_COMPARISON_SOURCES` contains eighteen comparison records plus the source owner's `NC-YAHUDA-7-3G` object, reused directly from `newton-chronology-sources.js`. It carries edition, locator, URL, actual reading/access metadata, rights, and source-body hashes. Six records retain inherited horizon passage receipts; twelve sources were freshly retrieved and selectively read in this lane. Newton's primary-source reading is credited to the separate source owner. Neither two translations nor two pages from one institution are counted as independent numerical validation.

`ESCHATOLOGY_COMPARISON_COVERAGE` states both represented and unassessed scope. `eschatologyComparisonById` is a pure lookup returning null for unknown IDs. Every comparison, textual-quantity record, provenance-gap record and coverage record has a citation string.

Accuracy verdicts are deliberately bounded. The Purāṇic duration values agree across the two named works, subject to their translation and transition conventions. The Greek/Roman sequences differ and remain different. The Maya date has one institutional source lineage, expressly not independent converter validation. The other unknown-date records report the selected passage's limited content rather than asserting that no date-setting tradition has ever existed.

Pike's preface identifies the Southern Jurisdiction and says readers may reject teachings; this does not make its comparative ancient-religion claims universally authoritative. The alleged letter is included only as a provenance gap. The institutional criticism was read, but neither an autograph, a catalogue shelfmark, the original Carr/Miller volumes nor a direct archival reply was inspected. We do not adopt its entire proposed genealogy as independently proven, reproduce the inflammatory alleged prophecy, or infer secret plans from it.

The Egyptian source choice is particularly limited: a late-antique Hermetic dialogue cannot substitute for pharaonic Egyptian religion. Similarly, the Sefer Yetzirah excerpt cannot substitute for the Zohar, later Jewish messianic calculations, or Hermetic adaptations. These gaps appear in the exported coverage ledger.
