# What can a calendar comparison identify?

## Forward calculation and inverse attribution

Let `t` be an explicitly defined instant, `z` an observer convention, and `C_j(t,z)` the date label in calendar `j`. A forward conversion answers what that convention calls the instant. It does not require any person to have used that calendar.

Let `B` denote an unobserved belief or decision rule and `D` the observed event dates. If the same observed dates can arise under two distinct rules, `B` is not identifiable from `D` alone. In particular, the conversion `D → {C_j(D,z)}` is deterministic and contains no new observation of a decision maker. Different date labels for the same instant cannot establish different causes, and equal solar/lunar dependencies cannot be counted as independent confirmation.

The Newton skill's backward/forward check asks whether a proposed historical cause reproduces the observations and whether alternatives reproduce them too. The Tesla skill's mechanism check asks what actually couples the proposed cause to the outcome, with defined inputs and a discriminating test. Here no measured physical or documented decision mechanism is established. Electrical resonance equations and Tesla's authority supply no missing premise for geopolitical prediction.

## Search size and selective matches

If one tests `m` hypotheses, the probability of at least one false rejection is at most the sum of the individual false-rejection probabilities. For valid tests each with size at most `α`, the union bound is `P(any false rejection) ≤ min(1,mα)`, without requiring independence. Only under independent tests, each of size exactly `α`, does the illustrative expression become `1 − (1 − α)^m`. This is a mathematical illustration, not a p-value for these calendar data.

Brute force makes the examined search space explicit; it does not remove researcher choice. Trying multiple founding dates, clocks, observers, house systems, calendar traditions, or definitions of a match enlarges that space. Choosing a winning rule after inspecting the same outcomes gives no held-out estimate of predictive accuracy. Opposed outcomes on similar configurations, and similar outcomes on different configurations, must be retained.

## Existing historical analysis

The country extension imports a frozen, independently audited descriptive study: 28 curated records, 23 source-event groups and 22 conservatively combined analysis groups. It is not a census of all wars or elections. The observer cities are comparison locations, not verified event venues or national birth charts. Three deliberately assumed clocks, 09:00, 12:00 and 18:00, expose some time sensitivity; they do not recover an unknown historical clock.

For a feature `x`, a marker's contrast is `x(event) − mean(x(control₁), …, x(control₈))`, where controls are the eight same-weekday offsets ±7, ±14, ±21 and ±28 days. A contrast is available only when the event and all eight controls are available. Shared cases are grouped before aggregation. These controls are not randomized, exchangeable non-events; overlapping conflicts and reused control dates remain flagged. Therefore the published contrasts are descriptive, not valid significance tests or estimates of war/election prediction accuracy.

The four unchanged editorial features are two Western phase preferences, a partial Jyotiṣa classification, and a Mercury-hour correspondence. They are not thirteen independently validated religious outcome models. The civil calendars remain a separate conversion table. At noon, 23 of 28 Jyotiṣa event values are available but none has all eight required controls; its paired aggregate is null. Null must not become zero, adverse, or a favorable result.

## What additional evidence would change the conclusion?

Attributing an actual practice needs source evidence: a contemporaneous instruction, diary, official scheduling rationale, authenticated correspondence or comparable record linked to the particular decision. A document about an individual cannot automatically establish a country's practice. Testing a proposed predictive model requires a frozen outcome definition, complete sampling frame, declared baseline, dependence handling and a genuinely held-out period. Neither requirement is met by matching dates after an event.

The reproducible product outputs are source-linked date mappings, documented chronology arithmetic and descriptive comparisons. No government belief classification, eschatological truth probability or best date for violence is produced.
