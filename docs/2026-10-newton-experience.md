## Round one: source-first working candidate

The existing source chronology and calendar instruments lacked one explicit route from a cited, conditional year argument to a separately assumed modern calendar sample. The implemented flow begins with a manuscript branch and displays the arithmetic, interpretive units, original folio, edition, reading scope, and caveats together. Two calculable arguments and four unresolved textual variants are selectable. The 1260-year lower-bound argument is not joined to the different 2300-year upper-bound argument. Incomplete lines remain uncomputable. The initial January 1, 2060 noon sample is explicitly a modern study assumption.

The engraved year circle maps Gregorian civil-day indices, with actual month lengths and up to four selected calendar transition rings. Three rings are selected initially; all thirteen calendar rows remain in the table. The plotted changes are first-observed daily samples, not precise religious boundaries, observances, or zodiac angles. The companion sky plate reuses the independently checked pure timeline SVG renderer. Its coordinates, ten bodies, twelve tropical house cusps, requested/actual system and fallback warning come from the same canonical frame.

A module worker computes the year survey and chosen frame. Replacement and cancellation terminate the worker and advance a response generation. Input changes preserve the prior rendered result with its original context and a visible stale notice. All calculation downloads and the contextual atlas link are blocked until a fresh response is accepted. No storage, provider request, geolocation or autoplay is used.

Owner Chromium checks at 1440, 390 and 320 pixels found thirteen calendar rows, eleven comparative records, no page-width overflow, no sub-44px buttons, and no page or console errors. Initial evidence was captured in `/tmp/newton-ui-r1-*.png` and `/tmp/newton-ui-r1-dial-*.png`. These are owner checks, separate from the independent QA receipt.

## Round two: evidence and visual corrections

The first actual render showed that short circular labels alone were difficult to read on narrow screens. A companion list now gives full selected-date labels for the visible rings directly beneath the circle. Chinese ordinal month labels are abbreviated as M01–M12 in the diagram while complete canonical text remains in the list, SVG titles and table. The selected-day measures shed inherited definition-list spacing, and mobile timeline labels receive a larger treatment. The decorative mark uses text presentation instead of a colored emoji.

Source-method review requested human-readable Hindu textual quantities rather than a JSON dump, and visible comparative coverage rather than an export-only scope statement. Both were corrected: divine-year units and unspecified epochs remain explicit; the selected eleven-record/nine-family scope and unassessed traditions are now visible in the limits.

Root review identified that a missing clock should not prevent exporting the research record. Unavailable-moment JSON/CSV and the complete survey JSON now remain exportable after a completed request; their canonical reason and unavailable rows are retained. Input edits, pending work and cancellation still disable these calculation downloads. A core-to-UI review also found that a previously valid atlas href could survive an unavailable sample; the link is now removed whenever the current frame is unavailable, stale or pending.

Moment JSON and CSV serialize the exact held canonical frame. The CSV includes explicit input context, civil-calendar rows, bodies, chart geometry, computus projections, complete source records and limits. Year JSON retains every sample, transition, solved event and source. Chronology/comparison source JSON is separate from the selected observer and remains available independently of stale calculation inputs.

## Round three: missingness and atomic result acceptance

Independent numeric/SVG review identified that a colored month interval could span an unavailable local-clock sample. The revised circle and linear timeline now add one dashed, crossed copper marker per unavailable date, with accessible date and reason. Period tooltips report both the civil-date interval and available sample count. The visible caption distinguishes these gaps from ordinary transition marks. The exact New York 2026-03-08 02:30 fixture has its marker at index 66 in both instruments; the Hebrew interval reports 28 samples across 29 dates. The independent reviewer confirmed these facts against app SHA `be30cde035aff22c8758a366f3d7ddab06ccac0e0db0eebcdbba327d1740f1db`; the noon control has no gap marks.

The source reviewer’s final provenance suggestions were applied: quotation or transcription view is attached to the displayed text, and the conditional anchor’s historical meaning is printed beside the arithmetic. Source data were not rewritten.

A new survey is now staged until its matching selected frame is accepted. Cancellation between those operations leaves the previous survey, events and frame together, rather than allowing a new survey to coexist with an old chart. Editing a date during such a replacement invalidates the pending operation. Ordinary same-survey date changes remain cancellable and discard obsolete responses.
