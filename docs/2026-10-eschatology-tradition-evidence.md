# Tradition evidence for the eschatology atlas

This selected catalogue distinguishes Jewish Kabbalah, Christian adaptations, Hermetic reception, ancient Ugaritic Baal material, the Church of Satan, The Satanic Temple, Thelema and Newton’s Christian chronology. It does not classify all of these as secret societies or combine them into one belief system. Publication history and a text’s description of divine time are different subjects.

The lane owns only `assets/js/core/data/eschatology-tradition-evidence.js` and this report. Source acquisition, extracts, the builder, claim ledger and checks are retained under `/workspace/analysis/eschatology-atlas/traditions/`. No browser, subagent, commit, protected-book crawl or shared registry edit was performed by this lane.

The next useful record for the Mathers issue is a scanned title/imprint and preface from the exact printing represented online. For TST chronology it would be a dated official founding instrument or contemporaneous statement; no founding year was filled from memory. For Baal it would be a named critical edition and the relevant tablet translation, not another modern allegation linking unrelated groups.

## Machine contract

The module exports frozen `ESCHATOLOGY_TRADITION_ENTITIES`, `ESCHATOLOGY_TRADITION_CLAIMS`, `ESCHATOLOGY_TRADITION_EDGES`, `ESCHATOLOGY_TRADITION_SOURCES` and `ESCHATOLOGY_TRADITION_LIMITS`. There are **21 entities, 16 claims, 10 edges and 16 sources**: eleven freshly acquired cited publications and five explicitly inherited selected-passage records.

Claims separate six date roles: `claimedDocument`, `statement`, `publication`, `transmission`, `target` and `historicalContext`. Each role declares day/year/year-range/unknown precision, documented/claimed/conditional/disputed/unknown status, a label and qualification, and a null clock. Unknown fields stay null. A year is never silently converted to January 1. Date-only records never acquire a noon event clock.

The Ugaritic historical-context range uses signed historical year labels −1550 through −1200 with `yearNumbering: 'historical-no-year-zero'`. The core owner explicitly accepted this as display/order metadata, not astronomical year numbering or Date/Julian-day arithmetic. Its calendar remains null. These numbers describe the source’s corpus context; they do not date a mythic event.

The only non-null `target` roles in this lane are Newton’s conditional 2060 lower bound and the separate 2370 upper bound. Neither is an exact day, and the two arguments must not be combined into a 2060–2370 forecast interval. The 1733 publication date belongs to a different kind of record. The current Thelemic calendar description supplies no target date for an aeon’s end.

Claim-level evidence includes a short extract where appropriate, what the source supports, what it cannot establish, contrary evidence, evidence dependencies and reading scope. An assessment of `supported` means the exact documentary or attributed proposition is supported by the identified packet; it does not certify a religious claim. `reviewStatus: 'unreviewed'` with `reviewMode: 'self-check'` is deliberate until independent review is recorded. Sources distinguish publisher authentication from retained-byte integrity. A hash does not establish historical truth.

## Typed graph boundaries

The one `influence` edge records Copenhaver’s explicit account of selected Jewish texts adapted by Pico. Its label and limits retain scholarly attribution and particular intermediaries; the edge does not claim that all Jewish Kabbalah became Christian or that the traditions share an institution.

`citation` edges record the named Mathers translation intermediary/Zohar selections, Westcott’s named translation, a modern scholarly article discussing Ugaritic stories, and the O.T.O. calendar’s reference to Liber AL. Mention or citation does not establish an original-language/full-book reading, a precise recension or common worship.

The four `comparison` edges have `asserted: false` and explicit modern descriptive labels: Pico’s two works, the title Baal and one referent, the two modern organizations, and Newton’s distinct bounds. They assert no causal or historical relationship. No graph edge links ancient Baal to a modern Satanist organization merely because a reader associates the labels.

The Pike source owner independently inspected an authentic passage naming “Sohar” and “Sephar Yezirah.” The shared entity IDs `TR-E-ZOHAR` and `TR-E-SEFER-YETZIRAH` allow that owner to encode narrow citation edges with their actual printed-page evidence. This lane does not duplicate that reading or infer that Jewish Kabbalah and Freemasonry are the same faith. No broader connected graph is manufactured merely to eliminate separate components.

Source-family and dependency fields distinguish repeated publication lineage from independent evidence.
