# Historical country/calendar methods — 30 October 2026

This module supports a bounded historical comparison of documented civic events. It does not select future dates, rank countries, infer a country’s true birth, optimize outcomes, or supply operational timing. The methods are source-qualified descriptions rather than one synthesized religious system. Astrology has no demonstrated predictive validity.

Authored files: `assets/js/core/data/country-history-methods.js` and `mcp/country-history-adapters.mjs`. The shared engine, curated event catalogue, UI and toolkit registration are separate panel lanes. Baseline: `e049fc9`, branch `feat/country-calendar-history`.

## Source and implementation contract

The source lane read the local shared [source contract](../.claude/skills/observatory-source-contract/SKILL.md), [accuracy check](../.claude/skills/accuracy-check/SKILL.md), [data-module instructions](../.claude/skills/add-data-module/SKILL.md), Lilly, Kabbalah, BPHS and Bṛhat Saṁhitā source profiles, and the Coptic, Hebrew, Indian, Islamic-civil and Persian calendar profiles. Profiles describe bounded work; they are not evidence of entire-book reading or human cultural authority. Canonical calendar code, existing source records, `HANDOFF.md`, calendar-method reports and inherited atlas/sky source reports were inspected.

`COUNTRY_HISTORY_METHODS` contains five named comparisons. `COUNTRY_HISTORY_HOUSES` contains twelve selected descriptive topics from Lilly. `COUNTRY_HISTORY_TASKS` contains four authored civic study tasks: anniversary, constitutional commemoration, historical public ceremony and education/archive study. `COUNTRY_METHOD_SOURCES` preserves source type, author, edition, locator, access depth, rights, review date and caveats. Every authored record carries a citation string. `countryMethodSourcesFor()` resolves only declared source IDs and rejects unknown identifiers. All exported data are deeply frozen.

The civic tasks ask for documents and precision; their requirements are explicitly editorial. They do not turn a historical rule into future scheduling advice. Public ceremony means recording a documented peaceful event, including whether the clock belongs to signing, announcement, ceremony or legal effect.

## Findings and distinctions

**Western/Lilly.** The selected chapter distinguishes natal/question contexts and named mundane figures. On pp.50–51 Lilly assigns the common people/general state to the first house specifically in eclipses, great conjunctions and annual Aries ingress. The tenth-house paragraph includes public offices and countries. The eleventh gives a London Common Council example. These are source-specific historical assertions. They do not prove that a declaration, constitution or independence ceremony is a unique national nativity. The data’s later civic analogies are labeled editorial, and selected summaries omit outcome rules. Regiomontanus, Placidus, whole-sign and equal houses remain alternative geometry conventions, not competing historical event records.

**Egyptian/Coptic.** Herodotus II.4, UCL and Britannica agree on the reported ancient civil structure of twelve thirty-day months plus five days. Herodotus’s statement about keeping seasonal uniformity is retained as his assertion, not adopted as a modern claim about an unintercalated year. UCL describes later Alexandrian/Coptic intercalation and explicitly labels its table Julian. UCL and Britannica’s compressed reform chronologies conflict; no adoption date is encoded. Ancient civil/lunar reconstruction still needs a period, epoch, calendar policy and independent anchors. A Coptic runtime display cannot supply these or establish Egyptian astrological interpretation.

**Hebrew/Jewish/Kabbalah.** Civil-midnight output is distinct from religious sunset dates and regional observance policy. A calendar conversion does not select a Sefer Yetzirah recension or a later Hermetic scheme. The named Maimonides paragraph gives direct evidence against presenting astrology as a universally endorsed Jewish practice. It is cited as one position, not a comprehensive legal ruling or replacement for other named sources.

**Indian/Saka/Jyotisha.** The existing `indian` converter is the Indian national Saka solar calendar. It is not a regional Hindu lunar calendar or Balinese Saka. Tithi, nakshatra, ayanamsha, sunrise-day and regional observance choices require separate conventions. The named Rao passage status and the project’s linear Lahiri approximation remain visible. No new numerical calendar fixture, Muhurta election or festival policy was introduced.

**Iran/Islamic/Persian.** Article 17 recognizes solar and lunar Islamic calendars and places government-office business on the solar calendar. That legal statement does not validate the application’s Persian implementation against every historical official rule. Tabular Islamic civil, Umm al-Qura and Persian runtime rows retain their own names and pending independent-display-fixture qualifications. No universal Islamic or Persian astrology is inferred.

## MCP boundary

`COUNTRY_HISTORY_MCP_TOOL` is a single standalone registration object named `workbench_country_history`. Root toolkit integration registers it. It imports the curated record IDs and calls `buildCountryHistoryStudy()` from `assets/js/core/country-history.js`; it does not duplicate calculations.

The JSON schema and direct-call validation allow only:

- `recordIds`: one to six distinct curated IDs;
- `houseSystem`: `regiomontanus`, `placidus`, `whole`, or `equal`, defaulting to `regiomontanus`;
- `includeDaySensitivity`: strict boolean, defaulting to false.

Unknown fields, arbitrary dates, caller charts, places, objectives, private payloads, duplicate IDs and coercion are rejected. Sparse record arrays are rejected. The complete canonical result is returned unchanged after a one-mebibyte UTF-8 serialized-size check; exceeding the bound throws and never truncates records, samples, warnings or citations. No host clock, browser storage, network, model inference or external writing occurs. Day samples remain assumed geometry and cannot recover an undocumented event clock.
